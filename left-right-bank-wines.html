Bordeaux  ·  Left Bank  ·  Right Bank

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

The two banks of Bordeaux, four estates a side. On the Left, a Calon-Ségur vertical from St-Estèphe and the two St-Julien Léovilles; on the Right, a Clos l’Église Pomerol vertical across two great vintages of the 1980s, a St-Émilion Grand Cru and Figeac.

What to look for

The Left Bank is Cabernet Sauvignon country: firmer and more structured, with cassis, cedar and graphite over grippy tannin. The Right is Merlot’s home: rounder and plusher, plum and truffle over velvet.

Watch the tannin as you cross the river (grip on the Left, silk on the Right), and read the two verticals for how each bank ages, from the mature 1982 Pomerol to the young 2019 Figeac.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Château Calon Ségur 2009

St-Estèphe, Left Bank · Third Growth

Left Bank St-Estèphe Opulent

Calon-Ségur, the northernmost classed growth of St-Estèphe, from the opulent 2009. Deep and sumptuous: ripe cassis, blackberry, cedar and sweet spice, velvety tannin and a warm, generous finish. The riper, more flattering half of the vertical.

Look for

2009’s ripe, velvety generosity, Left Bank Cabernet at its most seductive.

2

Château Calon Ségur 2012

St-Estèphe, Left Bank · Third Growth

Left Bank St-Estèphe Classical

The same estate three years on, from the softer, earlier 2012. Fresher and more classical: cassis and dark cherry, graphite and a savoury, cedary edge, with firmer tannin and brisk acidity. The structured foil to the 2009.

Look for

2012’s firmer, fresher frame against the plush 2009, one estate across two seasons.

3

Château Léoville Barton 2015

St-Julien, Left Bank · Second Growth

Left Bank St-Julien Traditional

Léoville Barton is St-Julien’s most traditional super-second, made in a firm, age-worthy style. From the ripe, charming 2015: cassis, blackcurrant leaf, cedar and graphite, with classic grip and a long, structured finish. Quintessential St-Julien Cabernet.

Look for

St-Julien’s balance of power and finesse: cassis, cedar and firm, fine tannin.

4

Château Léoville Poyferré 2016

St-Julien, Left Bank · Second Growth

Left Bank St-Julien Modern

A second Léoville, Poyferré, in a richer and more polished register, from the outstanding 2016. Concentrated and velvety: ripe blackcurrant, violet, mocha and spice, with fine-grained tannin and superb length. The two Léovilles show St-Julien’s traditional and modern faces.

Look for

Poyferré’s richer polish beside Barton’s classicism, and 2016’s velvety power.

5

Clos l’Église 1982

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot-led

Right Bank Pomerol Mature

Clos l’Église is a tiny Pomerol estate, and this is the elder of the Right Bank vertical, from the legendary 1982, one of Bordeaux’s greatest vintages. Fully mature yet still deep: dried plum, fig, truffle, leather and sweet earth, the flesh of a ripe year carried on soft, resolved tannin and a long, savoury finish.

Look for

A great vintage at full maturity: fig, truffle and sweet earth over silky, resolved tannin.

6

Clos l’Église 1988

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot-led

Right Bank Pomerol Vertical

The same estate six years on, from the firmer, more classical 1988. Also fully mature but tighter-boned: dried cherry, truffle, tobacco and forest floor, with a savoury, cedary edge over resolved tannin. Poured beside the 1982, the classic year against the ripe one.

Look for

The Pomerol vertical: 1988’s classical restraint beside the 1982’s riper flesh.

7

Château Grand Mayne 2016

St-Émilion, Right Bank · Grand Cru Classé

Right Bank St-Émilion Plush

Château Grand Mayne, a reliable St-Émilion Grand Cru Classé, from the outstanding 2016. Plush and generous: ripe plum, black cherry, cocoa and sweet spice, with round, velvety tannin and a fresh lift. Classic Right Bank Merlot in a great year.

Look for

Merlot’s velvet: plush plum and cocoa with round tannin, a Right Bank benchmark.

8

Château Figeac 2019

St-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé

Right Bank St-Émilion Cabernet-heavy

Château Figeac is St-Émilion’s great outlier, unusually rich in Cabernet, and now a Premier Grand Cru Classé A. From the superb 2019: cassis, cedar, graphite and dark cherry, more Left Bank in cut than its neighbours, with fine tannin and long, fresh length. The finale, a Right Bank wine that bridges both banks.

Look for

Figeac’s Left-Bank streak: cedar and cassis on the Right Bank, the theme’s bridge.
