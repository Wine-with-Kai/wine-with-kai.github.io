Napa Valley  ·  Cabernet  ·  Blind

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

Eight bottles, six vintages, one grape: a portrait of Napa Valley Cabernet from a single table. Every wine here is Cabernet Sauvignon or a Cabernet-led blend, yet no two are built the same way. Between them they take in the valley floor and the mountain benches, the historic Rutherford houses and the modern cult labels, and vintages stretching from a mature 2012 back to the ripe, exuberant 2019s.

They are set out below in one simple order, by vintage, oldest to youngest, so you have the full field in front of you. But on the night the wines are poured blind, in undisclosed order, with nothing on the glass to tell you the label or the year. The notes here are for afterwards as much as before: read them now for the lay of the land, then again once the bottles are revealed.

What to look for

Napa Cabernet has a house accent of ripe blackcurrant and dark berry, a warmth the cooler corners of Bordeaux rarely reach. But the game tonight is telling these eight apart, and three things are worth chasing in each glass.

Where it was grown. Mountain fruit (Antica, from Atlas Peak) tends to run darker, firmer and more mineral; valley-floor and bench sites (Stag’s Leap, Rutherford) lean rounder and more perfumed. How it was blended. Some are nearly pure Cabernet; Opus One and Orin Swift’s Papillon fold in the other Bordeaux grapes for breadth and lift. And the finish. Younger, riper wines tend to close sweet and plush with soft tannin; the more classical bottles, and the mature 2012, finish drier, savoury and longer, with structure that carries on after the fruit fades. None of it is a rule, and at least one wine will break your read. That is the point.

Each note is drawn from the winery’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes for that specific wine and vintage. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of one hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Opus One 2012

Oakville, Napa Valley · Mondavi and Baron Philippe de Rothschild

Oakville Bordeaux blend Mature · Polished

The oldest wine on the table: the Napa and Bordeaux joint venture founded in 1979, and the one true five-grape blend of the flight, 79% Cabernet Sauvignon with Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot, Merlot and a touch of Malbec, aged eighteen months in French oak. The 2012 is a generous California vintage now showing beautifully: blackberry, cassis and forest floor with tobacco leaf, graphite and cedar, then a layered, precise palate of dark cherry, mocha and anise. Silky tannins, a polished texture and a long, graceful finish.

Community Vivino 4.6★ CellarTracker 94.4
Look for

The gift of age: tertiary notes of cedar, tobacco and forest floor beside the fruit, and a seamlessness the younger wines cannot fake. If a glass smells older and more “claret” than the rest, this is your suspect.

2

Vaso, Cabernet Sauvignon 2014

Napa Valley · by Dana Estates

Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon Ripe · Supple

The second-label Cabernet from Dana Estates, one of the valley’s serious small houses, from a warm, early 2014 vintage that gave ripe, generous wines. Generous and nicely built, with classic aromas and flavours of bright currant and gentle oak, lovely berry-and-chocolate character, soft tannins and a fine, precise texture, medium to full-bodied and pretty. For all its early charm it keeps a core of freshness and the structure to hold a decade.

Community Vivino 4.4★ CellarTracker 93.8
Look for

Easy, open generosity: ripe fruit and soft tannin, with a little more mellowing than the youngest wines but less savoury age than the 2012.

3

Joseph Phelps, Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon 2016

Estate Grown, Napa Valley · the house behind Insignia

Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon Classic · Seamless

From one of the valley’s benchmark estates, drawn across its own vineyards in a celebrated, balanced 2016 vintage. Deep garnet-purple, it sings of crushed blackcurrant, blackberry and blueberry with cigar box, menthol and baking spice; the palate is a seamless, medium-bodied effort with a genuinely Bordeaux-like frame of cassis, leafy herb, green tobacco and violet over a fine graphite line. Built to reward another decade and more in the cellar.

Community Vivino 4.4★ CellarTracker 91.4
Look for

Poise and proportion, nothing exaggerated. The most “claret-like” of the younger wines, and a study in restraint that can read older than it is.

4

Beaulieu Vineyard, Georges de Latour Private Reserve 2017

Rutherford, Napa Valley · first made in 1936

Rutherford Cabernet Sauvignon Historic · Age-worthy

The most historic wine on the table: the reserve André Tchelistcheff shaped into the template for age-worthy Napa Cabernet, made since 1936. The 2017 is ripe, fruit-forward and voluptuous: brawny blackberry, dark plum and cassis with violet and black-pepper spice, then a rich, full palate of mocha and molasses framed by new French oak. Its calling card is the signature “Rutherford dust,” a round, cocoa-powder tannin that lends firm presence and a long, savoury finish.

Community Vivino 4.5★ CellarTracker 93.8
Look for

That famous dusty, cocoa-powder tannin on the finish, the taste of Rutherford. If a glass grips with a fine, earthy chalkiness, look here.

5

Stag’s Leap Wine Cellars, Artemis 2018

Napa Valley, with its heart in the Stags Leap District

Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon Elegant · Aromatic

The estate whose 1973 Cabernet famously bettered Bordeaux at the 1976 Judgment of Paris, and “Artemis” is its Napa Valley Cabernet, named for the Greek goddess of the hunt. The 2018 comes from a long, even growing season, and it shows the vintage’s fresher, more elegant register: expressive aromas of ripe raspberry, dark cherry and blackberry, with cedar, spice and, as it opens, violets, nutmeg and truffle. Medium-bodied and finely textured, with present but polished tannins and a savoury close.

Community Vivino 4.5★ CellarTracker 90.2
Look for

Perfume and lift rather than sheer weight: the most aromatic, red-fruited glass of the field, lighter on its feet than the powerhouses.

6

Antica, Mountain Select 2019

Atlas Peak, Napa Valley · the Antinori family’s California estate

Atlas Peak Mountain-grown Dark · Structured

A selection of high-elevation mountain blocks on Atlas Peak from the Antinori family, six centuries of Tuscan winemaking, planted above the Napa fog. The 2019 is juicy and inviting, with succulent plum, cherry and boysenberry lined with violet and red liquorice; critics find blackberry jam, spice, new leather and chocolate in an inky frame, balanced by tobacco, cedar, earth and silky tannins with real length.

Community Vivino 4.3★ CellarTracker 91.2
Look for

The mountain signature: darker fruit, a firmer, more mineral grip and a cool freshness the valley-floor wines do not quite have.

7

Caymus, Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon 2019

Napa Valley · the Wagner family, since 1972

Napa Valley Cabernet Sauvignon Lush · Crowd-pleaser

The valley’s great crowd-pleaser: the Wagner family’s benchmark, made since 1972 in a rich, showy style. Dark and near-opaque, it is fruit-driven and creamy: warmed vanilla, plum and boysenberry purée with melted black liquorice and a swirl of mocha on the finish. The palate is lush and rounded, with soft, barely-there tannins and a smooth glide of cassis and blackberry jam.

Community Vivino 4.6★ CellarTracker 89.5
Look for

Pure hedonism: sweet, creamy fruit and a velvety, low-tannin finish. The softest, most immediately pleasurable glass, and often the easiest to pick out.

8

Orin Swift, Papillon 2019

Napa Valley · Dave Phinney’s red blend

Napa Valley Five-grape blend Opulent · Bold

Dave Phinney’s theatrical Napa red, “Papillon,” the butterfly: a full house of all five Bordeaux grapes (Cabernet Sauvignon, Malbec, Merlot, Petit Verdot, Cabernet Franc), aged thirteen months in French oak. It pours an almost neon crimson into a dark core, opening with blackberry, kirsch and coastal sage over ripe pluot and roasted fig, then a more elegant palate of black plum, blackcurrant and toffee with muddled mint and orange peel, closing on ripe but firm tannin.

Community Vivino 4.5★ CellarTracker 92.3
Look for

Sheer depth of colour and the breadth the blend brings: more moving parts than the single-varietal wines, unashamedly opulent, with a firm tannic edge under the plush.
