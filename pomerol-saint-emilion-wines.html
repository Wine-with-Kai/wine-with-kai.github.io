Bordeaux  ·  Pomerol  ·  Saint-Émilion

The Wines notes for the table

Why these ten

The two great appellations of the Right Bank in five pairs from five vintages. Four pairs set a Saint-Émilion against a Pomerol of the same year: Ausone and Trotanoy in 1986, Cheval Blanc and Petrus in 1988, Angélus and L’Évangile in 1993, Figeac and Vieux Château Certan in 1996. In between, the ripe 1990 sets Saint-Émilion against itself: Canon-La Gaffelière from the foot of the slope, Beau-Séjour Bécot from the limestone plateau above.

What to look for

Pomerol sits on a low plateau of clay and gravel, and its Merlot tends to be round, dense and velvety: plum, truffle and iron. Saint-Émilion is more varied, from the limestone côtes around the village to the gravel by the Pomerol border, and gives more lift, more Cabernet Franc perfume and a cooler, chalkier line.

Poured blind, try to call each pair before it is revealed: which glass has the clay’s breadth, which the limestone’s grip? The two border estates, Cheval Blanc and Figeac, are the hardest to place.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Château Ausone 1986

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé A (until 2021)

Saint-Émilion Limestone Pair one

Ausone is the jewel of the limestone côtes, a small estate on the slope just below the village, its vines roughly half Cabernet Franc and half Merlot. 1986 was a firm, tannic vintage that opened slowly: dried cherry, cedar, mint and crushed stone, firm, chalky grip and a long, mineral finish.

Look for

Limestone’s signature: a cool, chalky line and Cabernet Franc’s lift, still holding its frame.

2

Château Trotanoy 1986

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot-led

Pomerol Clay Pair one

Trotanoy, owned by the Moueix family since 1953, sits on a slope of dense clay and gravel west of Petrus, and is mostly Merlot. It is one of Pomerol’s most powerful, long-lived wines. In 1986, a year of firm tannin, expect plum, black truffle, iron and leather, broad and dense, the tannin softened now to suede.

Look for

Pomerol’s weight against Ausone’s line: rounder fruit, iron and truffle from the clay.

3

Château Cheval Blanc 1988

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé A (until 2021)

Saint-Émilion Gravel Pair two

Cheval Blanc lies on Saint-Émilion’s gravel and clay plateau, right on the Pomerol border, and is led by Cabernet Franc rather than Merlot. The structured 1988 is classical and fragrant: red and black fruit, violet, tobacco and cedar, with fine, lifted tannin and a savoury, mature finish.

Look for

A Saint-Émilion that drinks like a Pomerol cousin: Cabernet Franc perfume over gravel.

4

Petrus 1988

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot

Pomerol Blue clay Pair two

Petrus is the most celebrated wine of the Right Bank: a small vineyard on a rise of blue clay at the heart of the Pomerol plateau, planted almost entirely to Merlot. From the classic 1988: dense and layered, with black cherry, plum, truffle and cocoa, a velvety texture over firm, ripe tannin and great length.

Look for

The clay’s depth and velvet, beside Cheval Blanc a few hundred metres across the border.

5

Château Canon-La Gaffelière 1990

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé since 2012

Saint-Émilion Foot of the côte Pair three

Canon-La Gaffelière lies at the foot of the côte just south of the village, on sand and clay over limestone, and has been run by the von Neipperg family since the 1970s. It carries an unusually high share of Cabernet Franc and some Cabernet Sauvignon. 1990 was a hot, ripe and celebrated Right Bank year: expect a lush, generous wine, plum, mocha and sweet spice with soft, ripe tannin, now fully mature.

Look for

1990’s warmth, framed by Cabernet Franc’s lift: ripe fruit, but never heavy.

6

Château Beau-Séjour Bécot 1990

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé

Saint-Émilion Limestone plateau Pair three

Beau-Séjour Bécot, the Bécot family’s estate, sits high on the limestone plateau west of the village, mostly Merlot with Cabernet Franc. It was famously demoted in 1986 and restored to Premier Grand Cru Classé in 1996, so this 1990 was made in the years it was out to prove a point. Expect dark plum, cassis, cedar and toast, with a firm, chalky spine under the ripeness.

Look for

The plateau against the foot of the slope: same vintage, same appellation, a firmer, more mineral line.

7

Château Angélus 1993

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé from 1996, A from 2012

Saint-Émilion Clay-limestone Pair four

Angélus, home of the de Boüard family, lies at the foot of the côte west of the village, on clay and limestone, with a high share of Cabernet Franc beside the Merlot. 1993 was a cool year hit by harvest rain; the best Right Bank wines made a supple, early-drinking style. Expect a mature, gentle wine: red plum, cedar, earth and spice.

Look for

How a light, rainy year reads at thirty: freshness and savoury detail over power.

8

Château L’Évangile 1993

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot-led

Pomerol Clay & gravel Pair four

L’Évangile shares the eastern edge of the Pomerol plateau with Petrus and Cheval Blanc, on clay and gravel, mostly Merlot with Cabernet Franc. The Rothschilds of Lafite took it on in 1990. Pomerol was among the successes of 1993, its early Merlot picked before the worst rain: plum, fig and truffle, round, soft and fully mature.

Look for

Pomerol’s early-ripening Merlot in a difficult year, set against Angélus.

9

Château Figeac 1996

Saint-Émilion, Right Bank · Premier Grand Cru Classé (A since 2022)

Saint-Émilion Gravel Pair five

Figeac is Saint-Émilion’s great outlier: its gravel croupes beside Cheval Blanc carry about two thirds Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc, far more than its neighbours. That suited 1996, a Cabernet year. Expect cassis, cedar, tobacco and mint, a firm, almost Left Bank frame and a fresh, long finish.

Look for

Figeac’s Cabernet streak in a Cabernet vintage: the most Médoc-like glass of the night.

10

Vieux Château Certan 1996

Pomerol, Right Bank · Merlot and Cabernet Franc

Pomerol Gravel & clay Pair five

Vieux Château Certan, run by the Thienpont family since 1924, sits on gravel over clay beside Petrus, and carries more Cabernet Franc than most of Pomerol. That gives it a perfumed, almost Saint-Émilion lift. From 1996: plum and black cherry, violet, graphite and tobacco, fine-grained and poised.

Look for

The Pomerol closest in spirit to Figeac: a fitting last pair, two Cabernet-minded neighbours.
