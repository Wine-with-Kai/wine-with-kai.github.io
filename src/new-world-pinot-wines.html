Central Otago  ·  Marlborough  ·  Four flights

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

New Zealand Pinot Noir in four flights of two, each pair from a single vintage so that place and hand do the talking. Flight one pours two 2015s with eleven years in bottle: Felton Road’s Block 3 from Bannockburn against Sato’s natural Pisa Terrace. Flight two stays in Gibbston for 2021, Two Paddocks’ First Paddock against Blank Canvas’s whole-bunch Anthem. Flight three keeps 2021 but crosses the South Island, Felton Road’s Calvert from Central Otago against Blank Canvas’s Escaroth from Marlborough. Flight four closes on two 2023s: Sato’s estate Sur Les Nuages, grown just above the Pisa Terrace, against Blank Canvas’s Upton Downs from the Upper Awatere.

What to look for

New Zealand Pinot trades Burgundy’s earth for riper fruit and sunlight, but the best sites keep freshness and detail. Central Otago, the world’s southernmost wine region, gives vivid, spicy, structured Pinot; Marlborough, better known for Sauvignon, a finer-boned, savoury style from its clay slopes and high Awatere.

Watch three things as the night moves on. The age, from the savoury 2015s to the primary 2023s. The place, from Gibbston’s cool edge and Bannockburn’s warmth to Marlborough’s clay and the high Awatere. And the hand: Felton Road’s biodynamic precision, Sato’s low-intervention wines, and Blank Canvas, who appear in three flights with three different sites.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

Flight one The 2015 vintage · Bannockburn against Pisa
Tasting focus

Two Central Otago Pinots from the warm, ripe 2015, now eleven years in bottle, from two very different hands. Felton Road farm Bannockburn biodynamically, and Block 3 comes from the limestone-rich corner of their Elms Vineyard: ripe dark cherry, wild thyme and a savoury depth arriving with age. Sato’s Pisa Terrace grows on schist and quartz gravels across the Cromwell Basin and is wild-fermented with minimal sulphur, by a couple who once worked vintage at Felton Road. Same year, same age: compare how each has matured, and whether the low-intervention wine has travelled further or stayed fresher.

1

Felton Road, ‘Block 3’ 2015

Bannockburn, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight one Central Otago Single-vineyard Mature

Felton Road are Central Otago’s benchmark, farming Bannockburn biodynamically; Block 3 comes from limestone-rich soils in the Elms Vineyard. From the warm 2015, now eleven years on: ripe dark cherry, wild thyme, spice and a savoury depth emerging with age, silky and long. Felton Road’s half of the 2015 flight.

Community Vivino 4.3★
Look for

Bottle age on a warm Otago year: dark fruit turning savoury, still fresh.

2

Sato, ‘Pisa Terrace’ Pinot Noir 2015

Pisa, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight one Pisa Natural Mature

Yoshiaki and Kyoko Sato left banking for wine, training at Bizot, Pacalet and Felton Road itself before founding Sato in 2009 on bought-in organic fruit. Pisa Terrace, gravelly loam over schist and quartz on the western side of the Cromwell Basin, was their founding site. Wild-fermented with a touch of whole bunch, 19 months in older French oak, minimal sulphur. From the same warm 2015 as the Block 3: red cherry and red fruit, now softening and savoury. The natural-wine counterpoint to Felton Road.

Community Vivino 3.7★
Look for

Same vintage, other hands: a low-intervention 2015 beside Felton Road’s, and what age does to each.

Flight two The 2021 vintage · Gibbston in two hands
Tasting focus

Gibbston is the coolest, highest corner of Central Otago, a schist gorge where Pinot ripens late and keeps its edge. Both wines are from Gibbston and from 2021, so the place holds still and the hand changes. Two Paddocks’ First Paddock is the leaner of the two: red cherry, cranberry, thyme and a whip of acidity. Blank Canvas make Anthem, from 21-year-old vines, with half the fruit in whole bunches, and it comes out deeper: ripe cherry, plum, spice and herbs. Watch what whole bunch adds: spice, lift and a firmer, more textured tannin.

3

Two Paddocks, ‘First Paddock’ 2021

Gibbston, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight two Gibbston Cool-climate Taut

Two Paddocks is the actor Sam Neill’s estate, and First Paddock its original Gibbston vineyard, the coolest, highest corner of Central Otago. From 2021, the same year as Blank Canvas’s Anthem from Gibbston: leaner and more herbal, with red cherry, cranberry, thyme and a whip of acidity, taut and savoury. The Gibbston foil, cool-climate and nervy.

Look for

Gibbston’s cool edge: leaner, herbal, high-acid Otago, the lighter side of the Gibbston pair.

4

Blank Canvas, ‘Anthem Vineyard’ Pinot Noir 2021

Gibbston Valley, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight two Gibbston Single-vineyard Whole bunch

Blank Canvas is the Marlborough label of winemaker Matt Thomson and Sophie Parker-Thomson, who buy from a handful of single vineyards they rate. Anthem is in Gibbston; the fruit is hand-picked from 21-year-old vines and trucked overnight, chilled, to their Marlborough winery. Half whole bunch, wild-fermented, 11 months in French oak (35% new), unfined and unfiltered. Deep ruby, vibrant and savoury: ripe cherry, plum, spice and herbs, with fresh acidity. Cameron Douglas MS gives it 95 points.

Look for

Two Gibbston 2021s side by side: Two Paddocks’ taut, herbal style against Anthem’s whole-bunch spice and depth.

Flight three The 2021 vintage · Central Otago against Marlborough
Tasting focus

The same 2021 again, now across the South Island. Felton Road’s Calvert, a second Bannockburn vineyard after Block 3, shows the warm, sheltered Central Otago style: fragrant red cherry, rose, exotic spice and cracked pepper. Blank Canvas’s Escaroth comes from a steep, dry-farmed clay slope in Marlborough’s Taylor Pass, at the top of the island: red and blue fruit, earth, game and fine-grained tannin. Ask which is the more perfumed and which the more savoury, and feel how the clay shapes the texture.

5

Felton Road, ‘Calvert’ 2021

Bannockburn, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight three Central Otago Calvert Fragrant

Calvert, a second Bannockburn vineyard after Block 3, is farmed by Felton Road and shared with a handful of top growers; this is from the outstanding 2021. Fragrant and detailed: red cherry, rose, exotic spice and cracked pepper, medium-bodied with fine tannin and vibrant length. The Central Otago half of the third flight.

Community Vivino 4.2★
Look for

Bannockburn perfume and spice, set against Marlborough clay in the glass beside it.

6

Blank Canvas, ‘Escaroth Vineyard’ Pinot Noir 2021

Taylor Pass, Marlborough, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight three Marlborough Clay Savoury

Escaroth, named for the lake town in The Hobbit, is a steep, north-facing, dry-farmed slope of ancient clay in Marlborough’s Taylor Pass, giving low yields. Twelve months in French oak. Red and blue fruit, then earth, game and warm spice; refined, graceful and deeply savoury, with pomegranate, red cherry and fine-grained tannin. Cameron Douglas MS gives it 96 points.

Look for

Marlborough Pinot from clay: savoury and fine-boned, beside Calvert’s Central Otago perfume.

Flight four The 2023 vintage · Pisa against the Awatere
Tasting focus

The youngest flight, both from 2023, from two cellars that bottle with a light touch. Sur Les Nuages grows on the Satos’ own farm on Mount Pisa, planted just above the Pisa Terrace vineyard from the first flight: one hillside, eight years apart. Blank Canvas’s Upton Downs comes from the cool, high Upper Awatere in Marlborough, planted entirely to Clone 777: violet, rose petal and wild red berries. Both are young and primary; look past the fruit to the tannin and acidity that will carry them, and think back to the 2015s to see where they are headed.

7

La Ferme de Sato, ‘Sur Les Nuages’ 2023

Mount Pisa, Central Otago, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight four Pisa Estate vineyard Natural

La Ferme de Sato is the Satos’ own farm, planted from 2016 directly above the Pisa Terrace vineyard, organic and farmed without chemicals. Wild-fermented without whole bunch, a long maceration, 20 months in French oak (22% new), unfined, unfiltered and bottled with only 15 mg/l of sulphur. James Suckling rates the 2023 96 points: fresh and dried strawberry, cherry and cranberry, orange peel, a hint of smoke and flint, medium-bodied with fine tannin and a juicy line. A callback to the Pisa Terrace in the first flight.

Look for

One hillside, eight years apart: the young estate wine from just above the Pisa Terrace poured in flight one.

8

Blank Canvas, ‘Upton Downs Vineyard’ Pinot Noir 2023

Upper Awatere, Marlborough, New Zealand · Pinot Noir

Flight four Awatere Clone 777 Fragrant

Upton Downs, grown by Tahi and Olivia Doonan in the cool, high Upper Awatere, is planted entirely to Clone 777 on loess and gravels over clay. Six-day cold soak, wild ferment, 11 months in French oak (35% new). Strikingly fragrant: violet, wild red berries, rose petal and forest floor, then cherry, pomegranate and savoury spice, elegant but firmly built on silky tannin and bright acidity. Rated 95 points; it closes the night.

Look for

Awatere’s cool, high fragrance against Pisa’s Central Otago warmth, two ends of the 2023 vintage.
