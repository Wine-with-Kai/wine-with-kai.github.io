Piedmont  ·  Barolo  ·  La Morra & Monforte d’Alba

Altitude, Terroir & Vintage

A simple deep dive into Barolo.

Eight Barolos in four flights of two, read three ways: the heralded 2021 in its youth,
the high-altitude crus of La Morra, and a traditional cru with real bottle age.

Flight one The 2021 vintage · La Morra against Monforte d’Alba
2021

Alberto Ballarin, Barolo Bussia

A La Morra house in Monforte’s Bussia · fragrant red fruit, fine-grained tannin

2021

Ca’ di Press, Barolo Perno

The Pressenda family, at home in Perno, Monforte d’Alba · soaring and taut

Tasting focus

Two stellar expressions of the lauded 2021, from cellars at opposite corners of the zone. Alberto Ballarin works from Bricco Rocca in La Morra and brings the La Morra touch, fragrant red fruit, floral lift and fine, architectural tannin, to its parcel in Bussia. Ca’ di Press is the Pressenda family’s cellar in Perno, Monforte d’Alba: look for soaring aromatics, intense depth, structural tension and a translucent finesse.

Flight two Vintage at altitude · one estate wine, two years
2020

Giulia Negri, Barolo La Tartufaia

A warm, expressive, approachable year

2019

Giulia Negri, Barolo La Tartufaia

A classic, cooler, structured season

Tasting focus

Giulia Negri’s estate Barolo, from her high vineyards in La Morra: Serradenari, up near 500 metres, with Brunate below. The same wine from two seasons, so the year is the only thing that changes. Set the warmth and generosity of 2020 against the cool, classic frame of 2019, and watch how vintage shapes Nebbiolo’s freshness and the build of its tannin.

Flight three The crus of Serradenari · the top of Barolo
2019

Giulia Negri, Barolo Serradenari

The whole cru · broad, layered and complex

2019

Giulia Negri, Barolo Marassio

Her highest parcel, just under 540 metres · chalk and flowers

Tasting focus

Serradenari is the highest ground in the whole Barolo DOCG, its slopes running from about 450 to 536 metres. Set the broad, complex Serradenari against Marassio, a single parcel at the top of the hill on limestone marl and fine clay, which gives a chalky minerality and a floral precision. Same grower, same year: only the parcel changes.

Flight four A traditional benchmark · bottle age
2014

Aurelio Settimo, Barolo Rocche dell’Annunziata

A cooler year, now open and savoury

2016

Aurelio Settimo, Barolo Rocche dell’Annunziata

A monumental year, deep and built for decades

Tasting focus

We close with traditional, long-aged Barolo from one of La Morra’s crown-jewel single vineyards, which the Settimo family farms around their cellar at Annunziata. The 2014, from a cooler season, has blossomed into delicate, savoury tertiary notes: dried rose, leather, truffle. The 2016, a legendary vintage, offers great depth and structure, with decades of life ahead of it.

Four flights of two, poured in the order shown. Please bring your own glasses: two sensory or Burgundy glasses each, so every pair can sit side by side. Seats are kept to no more than twelve.
