Nuits  ·  Beaune  ·  Chalonnaise

The Wines notes for the table

Why these seven

Burgundy off the Grand Cru path, across three districts. A 2017 Chambolle pair opens the Côte de Nuits; an old-vine village Chambolle and a Morey premier cru follow; a rare red Blagny stands in for the Côte de Beaune; and two vintages of Thénard’s Givry close from the Côte Chalonnaise.

What to look for

Pinot Noir is transparent to place, and this flight is a lesson in it. Chambolle-Musigny is the perfumed, silky voice of the Côte de Nuits; Morey firmer and darker; Blagny a wild, rustic red from the Beaune hills; Givry earthier and better value from the Chalonnaise.

Watch how commune and climat move the wine more than the grape does, and follow the two verticals (the 2017 Chambolle pair, the 2013 and 2018 Givrys) for the swing of the seasons.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Roblot-Marchand, Chambolle-Musigny 1er ‘Les Fuées’ 2017

Côte de Nuits · Pinot Noir

Chambolle 1er Les Fuées Perfumed

Chambolle-Musigny is the most fragrant commune of the Côte de Nuits, and Les Fuées a premier cru on the Morey border that lends a little extra structure. From the charming 2017: rose, red cherry, raspberry and violets, silky and mid-weight, with fine tannin and bright acidity. Classic, perfumed Chambolle.

Look for

Chambolle’s signature: rose and red fruit, silk and lift.

2

Albert Bichot, Chambolle-Musigny 1er ‘Les Chabiots’ 2017

Côte de Nuits · Pinot Noir

Chambolle 1er Les Chabiots Vertical pair

The same commune and vintage, a neighbouring premier cru. Les Chabiots sits mid-slope and shows a shade darker and firmer than Les Fuées: black cherry, undergrowth and spice over silky tannin. Poured side by side, two climats of one perfumed village in a single year.

Look for

Climat against climat: Les Chabiots’ slightly darker, firmer profile beside Les Fuées.

3

Dominique Laurent, Chambolle-Musigny Vieilles Vignes 2019

Côte de Nuits · Pinot Noir

Chambolle village Old-vine Ripe

Dominique Laurent, a Nuits négociant famed for old vines and bold oak, in a village Chambolle from the ripe, acclaimed 2019. Fuller and more sumptuous than the 2017s: ripe red and black cherry, sweet spice and toast, generous fruit and supple tannin. Village in name, but rich in cut.

Look for

Old-vine density and 2019 ripeness, carrying Laurent’s signature spicy oak.

4

Lignier-Michelot, Morey-St-Denis 1er ‘En la Rue de Vergy’ 2020

Côte de Nuits · Pinot Noir

Morey 1er Pinot Noir Structured

Morey-Saint-Denis makes the Côte de Nuits’ firmer, darker reds, and Lignier-Michelot are a reliable family domaine; En la Rue de Vergy is a high, cool premier cru above the village. From the superb 2020: dark cherry, iron, forest floor and spice, concentrated and structured yet fresh. The most serious wine of the flight.

Look for

Morey’s firmer, darker frame and 2020’s concentration, a step up in structure.

5

Joseph Drouhin, Blagny 1er ‘La Pièce sous le Bois’ 2022

Côte de Beaune · Pinot Noir

Blagny 1er Rare red Wild

Blagny is one of Burgundy’s rarities, a red-only appellation on the wooded hill above Meursault, and La Pièce sous le Bois its best-known premier cru. From Joseph Drouhin in the outstanding 2022: bright wild strawberry, cranberry, herbs and a rustic, earthy edge, fresh and sappy with fine tannin. A curiosity, and a delicious one.

Look for

A rare red Blagny: wild, herbal red fruit from the Beaune hills.

6

Domaine Thénard, Givry 1er ‘Clos Saint Pierre’ 2013

Côte Chalonnaise · Pinot Noir

Givry 1er Chalonnaise Earthy

Domaine Thénard are a historic Givry estate, and Clos Saint Pierre a premier cru from the Côte Chalonnaise, Burgundy’s earthier and better-value district. From the fresh, classical 2013: red cherry, cranberry, earth and a touch of game, medium-bodied with brisk acidity and light, grippy tannin. Honest, savoury Pinot.

Look for

The Chalonnaise voice: earthy, savoury red fruit and brisk acidity at village-plus weight.

7

Domaine Thénard, Givry 1er ‘Clos Saint Pierre’ 2018

Côte Chalonnaise · Pinot Noir

Givry 1er Chalonnaise Ripe

The same premier cru, five years on, from the ripe, generous 2018. Rounder and more fruit-forward than the 2013: riper red and black cherry, spice and a softer, earthy finish. A gentle vertical to close, the Chalonnaise in cool and warm guise.

Look for

2018’s warmth against 2013’s freshness, the same Givry premier cru either side.
