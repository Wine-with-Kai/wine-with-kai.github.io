Piedmont  ·  Nebbiolo  ·  the Langhe

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

One grape, Nebbiolo, and one wine, Barolo, read across four communes of the Langhe. From La Morra in the west, the most perfumed corner: Vietti’s benchmark Brunate, Cascina Ballarin’s Tre Ciabot, and two wines from Giulia Negri’s high Serradenari vineyard. From Castiglione Falletto at the centre, a mature Paolo Scavino Bric del Fiasc beside the traditional Roagna Pira. From Monforte d’Alba, Elio Grasso’s Gavarini Chiniera. And from Serralunga d’Alba in the east, on the sternest soils of all, a Luigi Pira. Same grape, same appellation: only the ground and the cellar change.

What to look for

Nebbiolo is all perfume and structure: pale garnet in colour, high in acid and fine tannin, built on rose, tar, dried herbs and sour red cherry. What changes across the night is not the grape but the commune. La Morra tends to the fragrant and more open; Castiglione Falletto sits in the middle, balanced and harmonious; Monforte adds depth and grip; and Serralunga, on its pale, iron-rich marls, gives the firmest, most austere and longest-lived Barolo of all.

Watch three things as you move east. The perfume, from La Morra’s roses to Serralunga’s tar and iron. The tannin, softer in the west and sterner in the east. And the vintage and the cellar: mature 2007 and 2010 wines against younger 2015 to 2018 vintages, and a traditionalist like Roagna beside more modern hands, so you can read the year and the winemaker as much as the ground.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Vietti, Barolo ‘Brunate’ 2010

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Classic vintage · Grand cru

Vietti is one of Piedmont’s great names, and Brunate one of the Langhe’s most celebrated crus, on the slope between La Morra and Barolo. 2010 was a cool, classic, structured vintage now counted among the region’s finest. Pale garnet, it opens on rose, red cherry, tar, mint and an iron-tinged, mineral depth. High acid and fine, firm tannin frame a long, savoury finish. A benchmark cru in a benchmark year: mature, but built to run on.

Look for

The classic 2010 profile: perfume, high acid and fine grip, from one of La Morra’s greatest sites.

2

Ballarin, Barolo ‘Tre Ciabot’ 2015

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Warm vintage · Rounder

Nebbiolo in a riper register, from the warm, generous 2015 vintage. Deeper garnet than the Vietti, with red and black cherry, dried rose, liquorice and a dusting of sweet spice. The palate is rounder and more giving than a classic year would allow: ripe fruit, softer tannins and a warm, gently structured finish. The more immediate, approachable face of La Morra, and a telling contrast in producer and vintage.

Look for

The warmth of 2015: riper fruit and rounder tannins, Nebbiolo at its most welcoming.

3

Giulia Negri, Barolo ‘Serradenari’ 2016

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra High-altitude · Classical

Giulia Negri farms Serradenari, among the highest vineyards in La Morra at around 450 metres, and works organically for a fresh, lifted style. 2016 is a great, classical vintage across the zone. Pale and perfumed, with wild rose, red berries, blood orange and fine herbs over a cool, mineral core. Bright acidity and elegant, chalky tannins carry a long, precise finish. Altitude and a top vintage in one glass: Nebbiolo at its most aromatic and detailed.

Look for

Altitude and freshness: high-toned perfume and fine-grained tannin from a great year.

4

Giulia Negri, Barolo ‘La Tartufaia’ 2017

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Warm vintage

A second Barolo from Giulia Negri, from the warmer, drier 2017. Rounder and more openly fruited than the Serradenari beside it: ripe red cherry, dried rose, sweet spice and a touch of earth. The palate is generous and earlier-drinking, with softer acidity and gentler tannins, though the estate’s high sites keep a cool, savoury lift on the close. The more approachable, sooner-ready face of La Morra Nebbiolo.

Look for

2017’s warmth: riper fruit and softer tannin, La Morra in a more giving mood.

5

Paolo Scavino, Barolo ‘Bric del Fiasc’ 2007

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Castiglione Falletto Mature · Landmark cru

Bric del Fiasc, from the Fiasco vineyard in Castiglione Falletto, is Paolo Scavino’s landmark wine and one of the crus that shaped modern Barolo. 2007 was a warm, early vintage, and at this age the wine is fully mature: garnet fading to brick, with dried cherry, rose, leather, tobacco, truffle and sweet spice. Still poised, its tannins resolved into a silky, savoury, tertiary whole. Castiglione Falletto’s balance of perfume and structure, shown at its peak.

Look for

Barolo with age: tertiary perfume, silky tannin and savoury depth from a warm, mature vintage.

6

Roagna, Barolo ‘Pira’ 2018

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Castiglione Falletto Traditional · Perfumed

Roagna is one of the Langhe’s great traditionalists, and Pira is their old-vine parcel in Castiglione Falletto. Pale garnet and ethereal, it leads with rose, dried herbs, tar and sour red cherry over a savoury, almost balsamic undertow. The palate is all lift and fine-grained grip: high acid, chalky tannin and a long, saline finish. A fragrant, classically framed Barolo from a fresh, perfumed vintage. Give it air, or the cellar.

Look for

Perfume over power: rose and tar, high acid, and Nebbiolo’s insistent fine tannin. The purist’s Barolo.

7

Elio Grasso, Barolo ‘Gavarini Chiniera’ 2016

Monforte d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Monforte d’Alba Structured · Classical

Elio Grasso is one of Monforte’s benchmark estates, and Gavarini Chiniera its Barolo from the Gavarini hill, aged in large casks in a restrained, classical style. From the great 2016 vintage, it is deeper and more structured than the La Morra wines: black cherry, plum, liquorice, tar and a fine dusting of spice. Full and firm on the palate, with abundant fine tannin and a long, mineral finish. Monforte’s power and depth, built for the long haul.

Look for

Monforte structure: darker fruit and firm, abundant tannin from a great vintage built to age.

8

Luigi Pira, Barolo Serralunga 2016

Serralunga d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Serralunga d’Alba Powerful · Age-worthy

Serralunga sits on the coolest, most iron- and limestone-rich soils in Barolo, and gives the zone’s firmest, most austere and longest-lived wines. Luigi Pira is a fine, traditional-leaning grower here, and this 2016 shows the commune’s signature: dark cherry, tar, dried rose, liquorice and a stony, savoury edge. Deep and tightly wound, with high acid and powerful, fine-grained tannin that will need years to unfurl. The sternest Barolo of the night, and the last word of the flight.

Look for

Serralunga power: tar, iron and firm tannin, the most age-worthy wine on the table.
