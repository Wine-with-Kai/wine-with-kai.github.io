Piedmont  ·  Nebbiolo  ·  the Langhe

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

One grape, Nebbiolo, and one wine, Barolo, read across four communes of the Langhe. From La Morra in the west, the most perfumed corner: Cascina Ballarin’s classic Tre Ciabot, Boroli’s grand-cru Cerequio and two wines from Giulia Negri’s high Serradenari vineyard. From Castiglione Falletto at the centre, Boroli’s delicate Brunella beside the traditional Roagna Pira. From Monforte d’Alba, Elio Grasso’s Gavarini Chiniera. And from Serralunga d’Alba in the east, on the sternest soils of all, a Luigi Pira. Among them, Boroli pours two crus, Cerequio in La Morra and Brunella, Barolo’s only monopole, in Castiglione Falletto: one cellar, held steady, so you can read two terroirs against each other. Same grape, same appellation: only the ground and the hand change.

What to look for

Nebbiolo is all perfume and structure: pale garnet in colour, high in acid and fine tannin, built on rose, tar, dried herbs and sour red cherry. What changes across the night is not the grape but the commune. La Morra tends to the fragrant and more open; Castiglione Falletto sits in the middle, balanced and harmonious; Monforte adds depth and grip; and Serralunga, on its pale, iron-rich marls, gives the firmest, most austere and longest-lived Barolo of all.

Watch three things as you move east. The perfume, from La Morra’s roses to Serralunga’s tar and iron. The tannin, softer in the west and sterner in the east. And the vintage and the cellar: vintages spanning 2015 to 2018, and a traditionalist like Roagna beside more modern hands, so you can read the year and the winemaker as much as the ground.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Ballarin, Barolo ‘Tre Ciabot’ 2015

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Warm vintage · Classic blend

Cascina Ballarin’s Tre Ciabot is not a single vineyard but the estate’s classic Barolo, a blend of parcels around La Morra, from the warm, generous 2015 vintage. Deep garnet, with red and black cherry, dried rose, liquorice and a dusting of sweet spice. The palate is round and giving: ripe fruit, softer tannins and a warm, gently structured finish. An open, approachable start, and the most immediate face of the night.

Look for

The warmth of 2015 and the ease of a village blend: ripe fruit and rounder tannin, Nebbiolo at its most welcoming.

2

Boroli, Barolo ‘Cerequio’ 2016

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Grand cru · MGA

From Cerequio, one of the most celebrated MGA crus in the Langhe, on the La Morra side. Youthful and concentrated, with a spiced, woody nose of milk chocolate and nougat over ripe raspberry and tea leaf, then pine forest floor and baked black cherry. The palate is compact and firmly built: prune, roasted coffee and clove framed by close-grained tannins and a dry, serious finish. The grander, more powerful of Boroli’s two crus, and one to reward patience.

Look for

Grand-cru depth and grip: dark fruit, coffee and firm tannin, the more age-worthy Boroli.

3

Giulia Negri, Barolo ‘Serradenari’ 2016

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra MGA · High-altitude

Giulia Negri farms Serradenari, the highest MGA cru in Barolo at around 500 metres, and works organically for a fresh, lifted style. 2016 is a great, classical vintage across the zone. Pale and perfumed, with wild rose, red berries, blood orange and fine herbs over a cool, mineral core. Bright acidity and elegant, chalky tannins carry a long, precise finish. Altitude and a top vintage in one glass: Nebbiolo at its most aromatic and detailed.

Look for

Altitude and freshness: high-toned perfume and fine-grained tannin from Barolo’s highest cru in a great year.

4

Giulia Negri, Barolo ‘La Tartufaia’ 2017

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo La Morra Warm vintage

A second Barolo from Giulia Negri, named for a truffle wood in the same high corner of La Morra, though not a registered cru of its own. From the warmer, drier 2017, it is rounder and more openly fruited than the Serradenari beside it: ripe red cherry, dried rose, sweet spice and a touch of earth. Generous and earlier-drinking, with softer acidity and gentler tannins, and a cool, savoury lift on the close from the estate’s high sites.

Look for

2017’s warmth: riper fruit and softer tannin, the same high La Morra ground in a more giving mood.

5

Boroli, Barolo ‘Brunella’ 2016

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Castiglione Falletto Monopole cru · MGA

Boroli’s Brunella is the only monopole in Barolo’s entire MGA system, a tiny cru in Castiglione Falletto they own outright, on the western edge of the Villero subzone. The more delicate face of the house: pale garnet and mid-weight, it opens slowly on wild rose, cherry, blood orange and a ferrous, earthy note, with light toast behind. Finely balanced, with silky tannins and ripe red fruit, plum and gentle spice. Fragrant and elegant, and less marked by oak than its stablemate the Cerequio: one cellar, two crus, side by side.

Look for

Barolo’s only monopole: perfume and lift, silky tannin under bright red fruit, the gentler Boroli set against its Cerequio.

6

Roagna, Barolo ‘Pira’ 2018

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Castiglione Falletto Traditional · MGA

Roagna is one of the Langhe’s great traditionalists, and Pira is their old-vine parcel in the Pira cru of Castiglione Falletto. Pale garnet and ethereal, it leads with rose, dried herbs, tar and sour red cherry over a savoury, almost balsamic undertow. The palate is all lift and fine-grained grip: high acid, chalky tannin and a long, saline finish. A fragrant, classically framed Barolo from a fresh, perfumed vintage. Give it air, or the cellar.

Look for

Perfume over power: rose and tar, high acid, and Nebbiolo’s insistent fine tannin. The purist’s Barolo.

7

Elio Grasso, Barolo ‘Gavarini Chiniera’ 2016

Monforte d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Monforte d’Alba Structured · Classical

Elio Grasso is one of Monforte’s benchmark estates, and Gavarini Chiniera comes from the Chiniera plots on the Gavarini hill, within the Ginestra MGA, aged in large casks in a restrained, classical style. From the great 2016 vintage, it is deeper and more structured than the La Morra wines: black cherry, plum, liquorice, tar and a fine dusting of spice. Full and firm on the palate, with abundant fine tannin and a long, mineral finish. Monforte’s power and depth, built for the long haul.

Look for

Monforte structure: darker fruit and firm, abundant tannin from a great vintage built to age.

8

Luigi Pira, Barolo Serralunga 2016

Serralunga d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Serralunga d’Alba Village wine · Powerful

Serralunga sits on the coolest, most iron- and limestone-rich soils in Barolo, and gives the zone’s firmest, most austere and longest-lived wines. This is Luigi Pira’s village Barolo, a blend of their Serralunga parcels rather than a single cru, and it shows the commune’s signature: dark cherry, tar, dried rose, liquorice and a stony, savoury edge. Deep and tightly wound, with high acid and powerful, fine-grained tannin that will need years to unfurl. The sternest Barolo of the night, and the last word of the flight.

Look for

Serralunga power: tar, iron and firm tannin, the most age-worthy wine on the table.
