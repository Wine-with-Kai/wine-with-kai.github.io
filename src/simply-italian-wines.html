Piedmont  ·  Tuscany  ·  the Veneto

The Wines notes for the table

Why these seven

Three of Italy’s greatest reds, read grape by grape. From Piedmont, two Barolos built on Nebbiolo: the traditional, perfumed Roagna and a rounder, warmer wine from 2015. From Tuscany, two Brunellos of Sangiovese: the modern, old-vine Siro Pacenti against the classical Canalicchio di Sopra. And from the Veneto, three Amarones of dried-grape Corvina: a Cesari single-vineyard in two vintages, plus a second house for contrast.

What to look for

Each grape makes a wholly different kind of red. Nebbiolo is all perfume and structure: pale in colour, high in acid and fine tannin, built on rose, tar and sour cherry. Sangiovese is savoury and red-fruited, with bright acidity, tobacco and dusty grip. Amarone is the outlier: made from grapes left to raisin over winter, it is dark, dense and high in alcohol, with candied fruit and a bittersweet richness.

Watch three things as you cross the country. The colour, from Barolo’s pale garnet through Brunello’s ruby to Amarone’s opaque black-red. The weight, from Nebbiolo’s lift and grip to Amarone’s velvet and warmth. And the house style: two producers each show a gentler wine and a grander one, so you can read the cellar as much as the grape.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Roagna, Barolo ‘Pira’ 2018

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Nebbiolo Traditional · Perfumed

Roagna is one of the Langhe’s great traditionalists, and Pira is their old-vine parcel in Castiglione Falletto. Pale garnet and ethereal, it leads with rose, dried herbs, tar and sour red cherry over a savoury, almost balsamic undertow. The palate is all lift and fine-grained grip: high acid, chalky tannin and a long, saline finish. A fragrant, classically framed Barolo from a fresh, perfumed vintage. Give it air, or the cellar.

Community Vivino 4.4★
Look for

Perfume over power: rose and tar, high acid, and Nebbiolo’s insistent fine tannin. The purist’s Barolo.

2

Ballarin, Barolo ‘Tre Ciabot’ 2015

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Nebbiolo Warm vintage · Rounder

Nebbiolo in a riper register, from the warm, generous 2015 vintage. Deeper garnet than the Roagna, with red and black cherry, dried rose, liquorice and a dusting of sweet spice. The palate is rounder and more giving than a classic year would allow: ripe fruit, softer tannins and a warm, gently structured finish. The more immediate, approachable face of Barolo, and a telling contrast in producer and vintage.

Look for

The warmth of 2015: riper fruit and rounder tannins, Nebbiolo at its most welcoming.

3

Siro Pacenti, ‘Vecchie Vigne’ Brunello di Montalcino 2015

Montalcino, Tuscany · old-vine Sangiovese

Brunello Sangiovese Old-vine · Modern

Siro Pacenti’s old-vine Brunello, from a warm, celebrated 2015, and the richer, more polished face of Montalcino. Deep ruby, with ripe black cherry, plum, leather, espresso and sweet spice. Full and velvety on the palate, dark fruit and fine oak carried by ripe, dusty tannins to a long, warming close. Powerful but balanced, and built to age a decade or more.

Community CellarTracker 93.6
Look for

Old-vine concentration and modern polish: dark cherry and spice over Sangiovese’s firm, dusty tannin.

4

Canalicchio di Sopra, Brunello di Montalcino 2017

Montalcino, Tuscany · Sangiovese

Brunello Sangiovese Classical · Savoury

One of Montalcino’s benchmark traditional estates, from the cooler northern hills. Classically styled and mid-weight: bright red cherry, cranberry, dried rose, tobacco leaf and a savoury, earthy edge. Fresh and taut for a hot 2017, with red fruit, orange peel and a firm, fine-grained tannic frame. Elegant and food-friendly, the more classical of the two Brunellos.

Community Vivino 4.3★
Look for

Sangiovese’s savoury red-fruited elegance and firm acid, a classical foil to the richer Siro Pacenti.

5

Cesari, ‘Il Bosco’ Amarone della Valpolicella Classico 2016

Single vineyard, the Veneto · Corvina and Rondinella

Amarone Classico Single vineyard Rich · Opulent

Cesari’s single-vineyard Amarone from Il Bosco, and the richest wine of the flight at around 15.5% alcohol. Dried rose, blackberry confit and baked plum give way to dark dates, chocolate and a smoky, tarry spice. Rich yet surprisingly poised, with a bright berry lift before the classic bittersweet twist and chalky tannins on the close. From a balanced 2016, the grandest glass here.

Community Vivino 4.4★
Look for

Concentration and dark dried fruit, with Amarone’s hallmark bittersweet finish. The fullest glass of the night.

6

Cesari, ‘Il Bosco’ Amarone della Valpolicella Classico 2017

Single vineyard, the Veneto · Corvina and Rondinella

Amarone Classico Vintage contrast Ripe · Warm

The same single vineyard, one year on, from a hotter and drier 2017. A shade riper and more opulent than the 2016: raisined black cherry, fig, cocoa and warm baking spice, the appassimento sweetness fuller here. Dense and velvety, closing on the estate’s signature bittersweet, chalky grip. Poured beside the 2016, a chance to read a whole vintage in a single wine.

Community Vivino 4.4★
Look for

The vintage difference: 2017’s extra ripeness and warmth against the 2016’s poise.

7

Tinazzi, ‘La Bastia’ Amarone della Valpolicella 2017

Ca’ de’ Rocchi, the Veneto · Corvina and Rondinella

Amarone Second house Glossy · Sweet-fruited

A second Amarone house for contrast, from Tinazzi’s Ca’ de’ Rocchi range. Corvina and Rondinella dried over winter give a dark, glossy wine of raisined plum, cherry liqueur, chocolate and sweet tobacco. Full and warming, with soft tannins and a rounder, more overtly sweet-fruited profile than the Cesari. A different cellar’s take on the same idea.

Community Vivino 4.3★
Look for

How a different house reads Amarone: glossier and sweeter-fruited beside the Cesari pair.
