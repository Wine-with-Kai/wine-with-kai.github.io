Piedmont & the Veneto  ·  Italy

The Wines notes for the table

Why these four

Four bottles, two houses, two of Italy’s noblest reds. From Piedmont, two Barolos from Boroli: the delicate Brunella and the deeper Cerequio, both from the celebrated 2016 vintage. From the Veneto, two Amarones from Cesari: the estate bottling and the single-vineyard Il Bosco. Piedmont’s Nebbiolo against the Veneto’s dried-grape Corvina, set side by side.

What to look for

These are two very different ideas of a great red. Barolo is all perfume and structure: pale in colour, high in acid and fine tannin, built on Nebbiolo’s rose, tar and red cherry. Amarone is its opposite: dark and dense, made from grapes left to raisin over winter, which gives it candied fruit, high alcohol and a bittersweet richness.

Watch three things as you cross from Piedmont to the Veneto. The colour, pale garnet turning to opaque black-red. The weight, from Barolo’s lift and grip to Amarone’s velvet and warmth. And the house style: each producer shows two expressions, a gentler wine and a grander one, so you can read the site and the cellar as much as the grape.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Boroli Barolo Brunella 2016

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo

Barolo Nebbiolo Delicate · Perfumed

Boroli’s cru from Castiglione Falletto, and the more delicate face of the estate’s Barolo. Pale garnet and mid-weight, it opens slowly on wild rose, cherry, blood orange and a ferrous, earthy note, with light toast and spice behind. The palate is finely balanced, with silky tannins and flavours of ripe red fruit, plum, eucalyptus and gentle spice. Fragrant and elegant, and less marked by oak than its stablemate, from a great year for Barolo.

Community Vivino 4.3★
Look for

Perfume and lift, and Nebbiolo’s classic tug of fine tannin under bright red fruit. The gentler, more aromatic of the two Barolos.

2

Boroli Barolo Cerequio 2016

La Morra, Piedmont · one of the Langhe’s great crus

Barolo Nebbiolo Grand cru · Structured

From Cerequio, one of the most celebrated vineyards in the Langhe, on the La Morra side. Youthful and concentrated, with a spiced, woody nose of milk chocolate and nougat over ripe raspberry and tea leaf, then pine forest floor and baked black cherry. The palate is compact and firmly built: prune, roasted coffee and clove framed by close-grained tannins and a dry, serious finish. One to reward patience, drinking well from now and holding for a decade or more.

Community Vivino 4.1★
Look for

Depth and grip, with new oak still settling into the fruit. The more powerful, age-worthy Barolo of the pair.

3

Cesari Amarone della Valpolicella Classico, Il Bosco 2016

Single vineyard, the Veneto · 80% Corvina, 20% Rondinella

Amarone Classico Single vineyard Rich · Opulent

Cesari’s single-vineyard Amarone from Il Bosco, grown on reddish marl, and the flagship of the flight at 15.5% alcohol. Dried rose, blackberry confit and baked plum give way to dark dates, chocolate and a smoky, tarry spice. Rich yet surprisingly poised for an Amarone, with a bright berry lift before the classic bittersweet twist and chalky tannins on the close. A terrific vintage, and the grandest wine of the four.

Community Vivino 4.4★
Look for

The fullest, most opulent glass: concentration, dark dried fruit and chocolate, and that hallmark bittersweet Amarone finish.

4

Cesari Amarone della Valpolicella 2018

Classico, the Veneto · Corvina and Rondinella

Amarone Corvina blend Savoury · Elegant

The Veneto’s great dried-grape red in Cesari’s estate bottling, made from grapes left to raisin over winter before pressing. Raisined cherry and plum on the nose with roasted spice, tobacco, leather and coffee; the palate runs to ripe blackcurrant, dried fruit and a savoury, peppery edge, with supple tannins and a long finish. Modern in style, showing grace over sheer power, and the more approachable of the two Amarones.

Community Vivino 4.2★
Look for

The appassimento signature: dried and candied fruit, a warm, glyceriney weight and a gentle bittersweet edge, but fresher and leaner than the Il Bosco.
