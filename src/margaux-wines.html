Margaux  ·  Bordeaux

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

One commune, six houses and three decades, poured in four flights that climb from the approachable wines through the classed growths and close on two verticals, Brane-Cantenac and d’Issan, each from 1989 and 2016. Margaux’s floral, silky signature runs through every glass.

What to look for

Margaux is the most perfumed and elegant commune of the Médoc: violets and red fruit, silky tannin, less overtly powerful than Pauillac.

Watch the fragrance and texture above all, the rise through the classification (cru bourgeois to third growth to second growth), and the two closing verticals for how a mature 1989 differs from a youthful 2016 of the same wine.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

Flight One

The approach

A second label and a cru bourgeois, fresh and supple

Tasting focus

Two wines from below the classed growths, and a gentle way into Margaux. Ségla, the second wine of Rauzan-Ségla, is all supple, early charm; Angludet, the grand vin of a well-run cru bourgeois, is firmer and built to age. Taste how far the commune’s violet-scented, silky signature reaches down the hierarchy, and how a fresh 2008 differs from a structured 2010.

1

‘Ségla’ 2008

Margaux · second label of Château Rauzan-Ségla

Margaux Second label Supple

Ségla is the second wine of Château Rauzan-Ségla, a Margaux second growth, from the fresh, underrated 2008. Supple and approachable: red and black cherry, violet, cedar and a light graphite edge, medium-bodied with fine, gentle tannin. An early, charming introduction to the commune.

Look for

A second wine’s charm: fragrant, supple Margaux for early drinking.

2

Château Angludet 2010

Margaux · Cru Bourgeois

Cru Bourgeois Structured Age-worthy

Château Angludet, the grand vin of a fine Margaux cru bourgeois long run by the Sichel family, from the legendary 2010. Structured and classical: cassis, blackcurrant, cedar and a floral lift, with firm tannin and excellent freshness, built to age. Punching above its rank in a great year.

Look for

A cru bourgeois over-delivering: 2010 structure and Margaux fragrance.

Flight Two

The third growths

Opulent, then finely drawn and perfumed

Tasting focus

Two third growths of the same rank, poured oldest first so vintage does the talking. Malescot St-Exupéry 2009 is ripe and opulent, deep and velvety; Marquis d’Alesme 2015 is finely drawn and floral. Same tier, same commune: set 2009’s warmth against 2015’s perfume and watch house style and year shape the wine.

3

Château Malescot St-Exupéry 2009

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Opulent Perfumed

Malescot St-Exupéry makes richly perfumed Margaux, and 2009 gave it opulence. Deep and sumptuous: ripe cassis, black cherry, violet and mocha, full-bodied with velvety tannin and a warm finish. The plushest, most opulent wine of the night.

Look for

2009’s ripe opulence dressed in Margaux perfume: violets over velvet.

4

Château Marquis d’Alesme 2015

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Floral Elegant

Marquis d’Alesme, a revived third growth in a floral, finely drawn style, from a 2015 that favoured Margaux. Perfumed and detailed: rose, red cherry, cassis and spice, elegant and mid-weight with silky tannin. Margaux’s fragrance in a charming year.

Look for

Finely drawn, perfumed Margaux from a commune-favouring vintage.

Flight Three

The Brane-Cantenac vertical

A second growth, mature 1989 against youthful 2016

Tasting focus

The first of two verticals, and a second growth across two eras. The same wine, Brane-Cantenac, from 1989 and 2016: the mature 1989, tertiary and sweetly perfumed, against the youthful 2016, still primary and firm. Twenty-seven years apart, with everything held but time and vintage.

5

Château Brane-Cantenac 1989

Margaux · Second Growth

Second Growth Mature Fragrant

Château Brane-Cantenac, a large and historic Margaux second growth in a perfumed, elegant style, opens its vertical with the hot, ripe 1989. Now tertiary and sweetly mature: dried black cherry, violet, leather, tobacco and warm spice, silky and resolved with a fragrant finish.

Look for

The mature half of the pair: dried fruit, leather and sweet perfume. Hold it against the 2016 beside it, and the d’Issan 1989 later.

6

Château Brane-Cantenac 2016

Margaux · Second Growth

Second Growth Vertical Age-worthy

The same wine, twenty-seven years younger, from the great, classical 2016. Primary and precise: cassis, violet, red cherry and cedar, with fine, firm tannin, bright freshness and real length, years from its peak. Where the 1989 shows dried fruit and resolve, the 2016 shows the raw material those tertiary notes came from.

Look for

The youthful half: fruit, grip and lift where the 1989 has dried, softened and turned savoury. Same wine, same house, a generation apart.

Flight Four

The d’Issan vertical

A third growth, the same two vintages to close

Tasting focus

The second vertical, and the mirror of the last: the same two vintages, 1989 and 2016, now from d’Issan, a third growth. The mature 1989 is tertiary and gentle; the youthful 2016 is classic and at full stretch. Set the pair against the Brane and read how house and rank, as well as time, shape the way Margaux grows up.

7

Château d’Issan 1989

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Mature Fragrant

Château d’Issan, a beautiful moated third growth, begins the closing vertical with the mature 1989 from a hot, opulent vintage. Now tertiary and gentle: dried cherry, violet, leather, tobacco and cedar, soft and resolved with a fragrant, savoury finish. Aged Margaux at its most graceful.

Look for

Mature Margaux: violet and dried fruit over soft, resolved tannin.

8

Château d’Issan 2016

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Vertical Classic

D’Issan again, from the outstanding 2016, closing the vertical begun by the 1989. Classic and complete: cassis, violet, red cherry and cedar, with fine-grained tannin, freshness and superb length. The estate young and at full stretch.

Look for

The d’Issan vertical closed: 2016’s youthful precision beside the mature 1989.
