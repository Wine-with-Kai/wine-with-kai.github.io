Margaux  ·  Bordeaux

The Wines notes for the table

Why these eight

One commune, seven houses and three decades, from cru bourgeois to Palmer. A d’Issan vertical bookends the flight (1989 and 2016), with Margaux’s floral, silky signature running through every glass.

What to look for

Margaux is the most perfumed and elegant commune of the Médoc: violets and red fruit, silky tannin, less overtly powerful than Pauillac.

Watch the fragrance and texture above all, the rise through the classification (cru bourgeois to third growth to Palmer), and the mature 1989 for how Margaux ages into grace.

Each note is drawn from the producer’s own description and critics’ published tasting notes. The community scores are the crowd’s verdict, not ours: Vivino on a five-point scale, CellarTracker out of a hundred. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

1

Château d’Issan 1989

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Mature Fragrant

Château d’Issan, a beautiful moated third growth, opens with the mature 1989 from a hot, opulent vintage. Now tertiary and gentle: dried cherry, violet, leather, tobacco and cedar, soft and resolved with a fragrant, savoury finish. Aged Margaux at its most graceful.

Look for

Mature Margaux: violet and dried fruit over soft, resolved tannin.

2

‘Ségla’ 2008

Margaux · second label of Château Rauzan-Ségla

Margaux Second label Supple

Ségla is the second wine of Château Rauzan-Ségla, a Margaux second growth, from the fresh, underrated 2008. Supple and approachable: red and black cherry, violet, cedar and a light graphite edge, medium-bodied with fine, gentle tannin. An early, charming introduction to the commune.

Look for

A second wine’s charm: fragrant, supple Margaux for early drinking.

3

Château Malescot St-Exupéry 2009

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Opulent Perfumed

Malescot St-Exupéry makes richly perfumed Margaux, and 2009 gave it opulence. Deep and sumptuous: ripe cassis, black cherry, violet and mocha, full-bodied with velvety tannin and a warm finish. The plushest wine of the flight’s first half.

Look for

2009’s ripe opulence dressed in Margaux perfume: violets over velvet.

4

Château d’Angludet 2010

Margaux · Cru Bourgeois

Cru Bourgeois Structured Age-worthy

Château d’Angludet, a fine Margaux cru bourgeois long run by the Sichel family, from the legendary 2010. Structured and classical: cassis, blackcurrant, cedar and a floral lift, with firm tannin and excellent freshness, built to age. Punching above its rank in a great year.

Look for

A cru bourgeois over-delivering: 2010 structure and Margaux fragrance.

5

Château Marquis d’Alesme 2015

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Floral Elegant

Marquis d’Alesme, a revived third growth in a floral, finely drawn style, from a 2015 that favoured Margaux. Perfumed and detailed: rose, red cherry, cassis and spice, elegant and mid-weight with silky tannin. Margaux’s fragrance in a charming year.

Look for

Finely drawn, perfumed Margaux from a commune-favouring vintage.

6

Château d’Issan 2016

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth Vertical Classic

D’Issan again, from the outstanding 2016, closing the vertical begun by the 1989. Classic and complete: cassis, violet, red cherry and cedar, with fine-grained tannin, freshness and superb length. The estate young and at full stretch.

Look for

The d’Issan vertical closed: 2016’s youthful precision beside the mature 1989.

7

Château Palmer 2017

Margaux · Third Growth

Third Growth First-growth quality Refined

Château Palmer is the third growth that drinks like a first, unusually rich in Merlot for Margaux. From the frost-marked but elegant 2017: violet, dark cherry, cassis, mocha and a silky, aromatic depth, refined and long. The peak of the flight.

Look for

Palmer’s silk and perfume, the estate that transcends its rank.
