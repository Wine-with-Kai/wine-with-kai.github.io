Piedmont  ·  Barolo  ·  Exploring Altitude, Terroir & Vintage

The Wines notes for the table

Why these ten

One grape, Nebbiolo, and one appellation, Barolo, explored across several dimensions at once: the heralded 2021 vintage in its youth, the high-altitude micro-terroirs of La Morra, mature, traditional cru ageing, and the balance of Castiglione Falletto. Five flights of two, each pair changing one thing so you can taste what it does.

Flight one sets two 2021s from Monforte d’Alba against each other, a La Morra house in Bussia and a Monforte family in Perno. Flight two pours Giulia Negri’s La Tartufaia from 2019 and 2020. Flight three climbs to Serradenari, the highest point in Barolo, for her whole-cru Serradenari against the single plot of Marassio. Flight four turns to Aurelio Settimo’s Rocche dell’Annunziata, 2014 against 2016, and flight five ends at the centre of the zone, in Castiglione Falletto: Giuseppe Mascarello’s Monprivato from 2014 against Roagna’s Pira from 2018.

What to look for

Barolo is Nebbiolo: pale in the glass but never light, with rose, tar, red cherry and herbs on the nose and firm tannin and bright acidity on the palate. Across the night, three questions are worth carrying from glass to glass.

Vintage. Flights two and four keep the vineyard and cellar fixed and change only the year, and flight five pours a second 2014 to set beside the Settimo. Warm years such as 2020 give rounder fruit and softer tannin; cool, classic years such as 2019 and 2014 give more acidity, more perfume and a firmer line. Altitude and soil. Higher vineyards ripen more slowly and keep their freshness; La Morra’s marls tend towards perfume and silky tannin, Monforte’s towards depth and power, and Castiglione Falletto, between them, holds the two in balance. Time. Young Barolo shows fruit and grip; with a decade in bottle, fruit turns to dried rose, leather and truffle, and the tannin softens.

Bring two glasses, sensory or Burgundy, so each pair can stand side by side. Taste across the pair, then back again: the second sip of the first wine often tells you the most.

Each note builds on Kai’s brief with the producers’ own descriptions and critics’ published notes. Signposts, not a script; trust your own glass first.

Flight one The 2021 vintage · La Morra against Monforte d’Alba
Tasting focus

Two stellar expressions of the lauded 2021 vintage, from opposite stylistic corners of Barolo. Both vineyards lie in Monforte d’Alba, so the contrast is in the hands: a La Morra house bringing its fragrant, fine-grained style to Bussia, against a Monforte family at home in Perno, with soaring aromatics, intense depth and structural tension.

2021 is already counted among the great modern Barolo vintages. A snowy winter and a cool April and May held the vines back; a long summer followed, warm and dry but never scorching, and the grapes were picked healthy in the classic October window. The wines have low pH, high natural acidity and ripe tannin: classically built Barolo after a run of warmer years, and wonderfully clear about where each one grew.

1

Alberto Ballarin, Barolo Bussia 2021

Monforte d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · a La Morra house

Monforte d’Alba Bussia Flight one

Alberto Ballarin, long known as Cascina Ballarin, is a small family house run by the Viberti family since 1928 from Bricco Rocca, a hilltop in La Morra just short of the Barolo border. Alongside its La Morra vines it owns a piece of Bussia, one of Monforte’s largest and most famous crus, on limestone and sand. The wine is fermented in steel with a long maceration, then aged for at least three years in large casks and barrique. Expect withered rose and violet, ripe red fruit, sweet spice and liquorice; on the palate it is enveloping, with silky tannin and a savoury balance.

Look for

The La Morra touch on Monforte ground: perfume and fine-grained tannin first, Bussia’s depth underneath. Does the house or the hill speak louder?

2

Ca’ di Press, Barolo Perno 2021

Monforte d’Alba, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · the Pressenda family

Monforte d’Alba Perno Flight one

Ca’ di Press means “the Pressenda house” in Piedmontese. The family has farmed around Perno since the early 1900s; the sisters Alice and Cristina came home in the mid-2010s and released their first Barolo with the 2018 vintage, to instant acclaim. The farming is nature-led and done largely by hand, with spontaneous fermentation, a traditional maceration in steel, ageing in neutral vessels and no fining or filtering. Antonio Galloni of Vinous gave this 2021 97 points, writing of soaring aromatics and silky tannins around a core of pure red and purplish fruit. Look too for clove and earthy notes, warm, enveloping tannin and a long, silky finish.

Look for

Tension and transparency: high-toned aromatics over real depth, a translucent finesse you can almost see through. Set it beside the Ballarin and feel the difference in grip.

Flight two Vintage at altitude · one estate wine, two years
Tasting focus

Giulia Negri’s estate blend from the high soils of La Morra, poured from two seasons. 2019, a classic, structured, cooler season, set directly against 2020, a warm, expressive and approachable vintage, shows how the year alone reshapes Nebbiolo’s freshness and the architecture of its tannin.

La Tartufaia takes its name from a truffle ground, a nod to the white-truffle woods around the estate. It is drawn from her high vineyards in Brunate and Serradenari, on loam and sand, and made the way she makes every Barolo: spontaneous fermentation, a long maceration of around forty days, then ageing in large Slavonian oak. It is the earliest-drinking of her Barolos, which makes it a clear lens on vintage.

3

Giulia Negri, Barolo La Tartufaia 2019

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Brunate and Serradenari

La Morra Classic vintage Flight two

A cooler, classic season, and a more upright wine. Rose petal, small red berries and a nutty note on the nose; the palate is silky, almost creamy, delicate and refined, with roasted almond and a hint of cocoa on the finish, all carried by fresh acidity and the salty minerality of the high ground. More reserved than the 2020 today, it has the frame to keep improving for years.

Look for

Line before roundness: brighter acidity, firmer tannin, more perfume and less flesh. Keep it in mind for the 2020.

4

Giulia Negri, Barolo La Tartufaia 2020

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Brunate and Serradenari

La Morra Warm vintage Flight two

A warm year, and the generous face of this wine. Open and expressive from the first pour: red berries, rose petal and crushed cherry and raspberry, with a savoury undertow of forest floor, tar, earthy clay and medicinal herbs, and a fresh lift of pine needle. There is a little grip, as a young Barolo should have, but the purity of the fruit makes it hard to resist. It gains a great deal from air, so let it sit in the glass.

Look for

The warmth of the year: rounder fruit and softer edges than the 2019, the friendlier face of altitude. Note how much fruit sits in front of the tannin.

Flight three The crus of Serradenari · the top of Barolo
Tasting focus

Both wines come from Serradenari, the highest ground in the entire Barolo DOCG, its slopes rising from about 450 to 536 metres. Compare the broad, complex expression of the whole Serradenari cru with Marassio, a single plot at the top of the hill on limestone and fine clay, which brings a chalky minerality and floral precision.

Giulia Negri’s great-great-grandfather bought Serradenari in the 1870s: one body of about fourteen hectares of vines and truffle woods, with the cellar at its heart. Giulia spent a formative spell in Burgundy and has run the estate since the mid-2010s, working it organically, parcel by parcel, with an almost Burgundian focus. Up here the air is cool and windy, and Nebbiolo ripens slowly and evenly, keeping its freshness and perfume.

5

Giulia Negri, Barolo Serradenari 2019

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Serradenari MGA

La Morra 450 to 536 m Flight three

The whole of the cru in one bottle, from limestone-rich marl with clay beneath. James Suckling found the 2019 extremely floral, all violets and roses, with orange peel and wild strawberries. The estate’s notes on the cru add plum, spice, truffle and black cherry with a dried-fruit undertone, finely integrated tannin and a light minerality. In a cool, classic year, expect Serradenari at its most aromatic and taut.

Look for

Breadth and layers: the whole hill at once, floral, fruited and savoury. Keep it in mind for the next glass.

6

Giulia Negri, Barolo Marassio 2019

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · a single plot on Serradenari

La Morra Just under 540 m Flight three

Marassio is Giulia Negri’s highest parcel, just under 540 metres on the western edge of La Morra, a cool, windy spot on limestone marl and fine clay. It ferments spontaneously, spends about forty days on its skins and then thirty months in large Slavonian oak. The 2019 is luminous garnet, with honey and summer flowers on the nose, then cherry, mint and truffle; Suckling found blackberries, flowers and lavender. It has power and well-integrated tannin, but its signature is structure and elegance: a firm, chalky line.

Look for

Precision over breadth: a narrower, chalkier line and more floral lift than the Serradenari. Same grower, same year, same hill, only the parcel changes.

Flight four A traditional benchmark · bottle age
Tasting focus

Traditional, long-aged winemaking from one of La Morra’s crown-jewel single vineyards. The 2014, from a cooler vintage, has blossomed into delicate, savoury tertiary notes of dried rose, leather and truffle. The 2016, a legendary, monumental vintage, offers great depth and structure, with decades of life ahead.

Aurelio Settimo has made only estate wines, vinified by tradition, since 1962. Since Aurelio’s death in 2007 his daughter Tiziana has run the cellar, and she calls herself a traditionalist: no barriques, a maceration of about twenty days, a year in cement, then two in large oak casks. Of the estate’s 5.67 hectares of Nebbiolo, 3.42 lie on Rocche dell’Annunziata, a south-facing amphitheatre below the hamlet of Annunziata, on Sant’Agata fossil marls unusually rich in silt. It is the source of the perfumed, silky La Morra style at its finest.

7

Aurelio Settimo, Barolo Rocche dell’Annunziata 2014

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Rocche dell’Annunziata MGA

La Morra Cool vintage Flight four

2014 was a cool, wet summer that tested every grower, rescued by a fine late season; the best Barolos came out elegant and perfumed, lighter-framed than usual and quicker to open. A decade on, this has moved into its savoury, tertiary prime: dried rose, leather and truffle around a succulent core of fresh and dried cherry and plum, with sweet hay and spice. The palate is slender and long, its tannins lithe and well integrated, with Nebbiolo’s grip still at the edges.

Look for

What a decade does: fruit giving way to rose, leather and truffle, and tannin melted to silk. The grace of a lighter year.

8

Aurelio Settimo, Barolo Rocche dell’Annunziata 2016

La Morra, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Rocche dell’Annunziata MGA

La Morra Great vintage Flight four

The same vineyard and cellar, two years later, in a vintage now held as a benchmark of the modern era: a long, even season without heat spikes, cool nights and a late harvest that brought fully ripe fruit with fresh acidity and great tannin. In this traditional style, expect more of everything than the 2014: darker cherry and plum, rose, tar and spice, real depth and a firm, fine frame still unwinding. At ten years old it is only beginning to open, so give it time in the glass. Of all ten, it may have the most years ahead of it.

Look for

The same hill in a different stage of life: power and promise beside the 2014’s grace. Which would you rather drink tonight, and which in ten years?

Flight five The heart of Castiglione Falletto · 2014 against 2018
Tasting focus

A Castiglione Falletto masterclass to finish: the supreme equilibrium of the commune, where the structural backbone of the Serralunga ridge meets the aromatic elegance of the western communes. The 2014 is a prized cru from the cooler vintage, showing dried flowers and a refined, almost weightless structure. 2018 began very wet, turned warm, and settled into a classic, sunny harvest; its best wines are aromatic and delicate, with early charm, soft, supple tannin and pure fruit.

Castiglione Falletto is a small commune at the very centre of the zone, with La Morra and Barolo to the west and Serralunga and Monforte to the east and south. Its soils mix the marls of both sides, which is why it is so often called the balance point of Barolo. Both vineyards here are held almost entirely by one family, and both families make wine the traditional way.

9

Giuseppe Mascarello, Barolo Monprivato 2014

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Monprivato MGA

Castiglione Falletto Monprivato Flight five

Giuseppe Mascarello e Figlio, with its cellar in Monchiero, is one of Barolo’s great traditional houses. The family has worked Monprivato for more than a century and holds just over six hectares, nearly all of the cru: a slope facing south to south-west at around 280 metres, on fine calcareous marl of marine origin. In 1970 Mauro Mascarello bottled it on its own for the first time, an early landmark among single-vineyard Barolos. The cool 2014 suits the site. Critics found alpine strawberry, rose, pomegranate, peach and ripe cherry, then woodland berry, wild herbs, violet and camphor; firm acidity carries it, with a mineral tension and an ethereal, almost weightless texture. Galloni judged it not an epic Monprivato, but a very beautiful one.

Look for

Two 2014s, two communes: hold it against the Settimo from flight four. Dried flowers, a weightless frame, and the poise Castiglione Falletto is prized for.

10

Roagna, Barolo Pira 2018

Castiglione Falletto, Piedmont · Nebbiolo · Pira MGA

Castiglione Falletto Pira Flight five

The Roagna family made their name in Barbaresco; in 1989 they bought Pira, a historic estate in Castiglione Falletto that they work as its sole owners. The vineyard lies on the south-eastern slope below the Rocche di Castiglione, sheltered by the rock above and by woodland, on alternating layers of white limestone, grey and blue marl and sand washed down from the Rocche. Roagna is famously traditional: long macerations and patient ageing in large casks, with the oldest vines, planted in 1937, kept for a separate Vecchie Viti bottling. Antonio Galloni of Vinous found this 2018 bright, focused and linear, a little light and nervy as the vintage tends to be, with tobacco, cedar, mint, dried flowers and chamomile.

Look for

Perfume and lift over weight: supple tannin and early charm, the open face of 2018 after the Monprivato’s poise. Set its freshness against the 2021s that began the night.
